Life and Contributions

John Field (1782–1837)
Legacy

Left an enduring legacy as a pioneer of Romantic piano music, particularly through his contributions to the nocturne genre.

Education and Early Training
  • Displayed exceptional musical (pianistic) talent at an early age
  • Studied at the Royal Conservatory in Dublin
  • Moved to London; mentored by Clementi

(Deeply influenced Field’s early piano works, especially his Three Sonatas, Op. 1.)

John Field9 / 21

Life and Contributions

Departure from London to Russia (1802–1837)
  • Field moved to St. Petersburg in 1802
Development of Personal Style
  • Began developing his own distinct romantic style, moving away from his earlier classical influences
  • Became known for his emotive and lyrical piano compositions, particularly his nocturnes
John Field10 / 21

Life and Contributions

Key Compositional Contributions
  • 7 Piano Concertos (1799–early 1830s)
  • Rondo, 2 Fantasias, and Character Pieces, including Polonaise and Mazurka (late 1790s–1830s)
  • 18 Nocturnes (1812–1836)
  • 7 Piano Sonatas and 2 Waltzes (1810s–1830s)
Pioneered Nocturne Genre
  • Lyrical melodies, expressive depth
  • Influenced Romantic piano music (e.g., Chopin)
John Field11 / 21

Compositional Contributions: Nocturnes

Key Features
  • Cantabile Melody: Song-like, ornamented
  • Dreamlike Quality: Serene, introspective
  • Broken-Chord Accompaniment: Flowing arpeggios
  • Rubato: Flexible tempo, expressiveness
  • Simple Structure: Simple ternary form
  • Subtle Dynamics & Legato: Nuanced expression
Innovative Techniques
  • Flowing arpeggios for a dreamy and atmospheric accompaniment
  • Intricate fingerings (i.e. 3-2-3-2 for scales, successive 4s for descending scale with 9 notes)

Frédéric Chopin16 / 21

Early Life & Prodigy Years (1810–1829)

  • Born in 1810 (Poland)
  • Started playing piano in age 4
  • First composition in Age 6
  • Early teacher: Wojciech Żywny (1816–1823)
  • Music studies with Józef Elsner at Warsaw Conservatory (1823–1829)

Frédéric Chopin18 / 21

Rise to Fame (1829–1831)

Vienna debut: August 11, 1829

  • First published work: Variations on Là ci darem la mano, Op. 2
  • Recognition from Robert Schumann: Score Review of Chopin’s Variations on Là ci darem la mano, Op. 2 for the music journal “Allgemeine musikalische Zeitung” (1831)
“Hats off, gentlemen! A genius... I believe Chopin to be the boldest, proudest poetic spirit of the time.”
